### Houthi Blockade of Bab el-Mandeb Strait Collapses Saudi Oil Exports, Draws Italian Warships

<!-- audio:top-story-1 -->
Saudi Arabia's oil exports through the Bab el-Mandeb strait have collapsed from 3.5 million barrels per day in early July to nearly zero by August, as Houthi forces consolidate control of territory along the waterway.<sup>[1](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/11/business/bab-al-mandeb-strait-houthi-global-economy)</sup> The disruption blocks an estimated 10% of global seaborne oil traffic, and Saudi Aramco says its East-West Pipeline has already reached its maximum capacity of 7.0 million barrels per day, leaving no buffer to absorb further losses.<sup>[2](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/17/from-yanbu-to-sohar-tracking-saudi-arabias-alternative-oil-routes)</sup> Asian buyers are being forced to reroute cargoes around Africa via the Cape of Good Hope, adding roughly 30 days of transit time and pushing up shipping and insurance costs.<sup>[3](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/energy/2026/07/26/saudi-arabia-shifts-to-suez-as-houthis-drive-bab-al-mandeb-oil-exports-to-near-zero/)</sup>

Italy has responded unilaterally, with its defence minister announcing the deployment of Italian warships to the strait to protect commercial shipping, a move that reflects frustration with the pace of a coordinated EU response.<sup>[4](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/18/italy-to-deploy-warships-to-protect-shipping-through-bab-al-mandeb)</sup> The deployment underscores how much of world trade and energy security now depends on keeping this single chokepoint open, and puts pressure on other European navies to follow Rome's lead.

> **Historical Parallel: Piracy off the Coast of Somalia** (2006–present)
> A previous crisis in the same waters — the Gulf of Aden and the Horn of Africa — saw Somali piracy escalate from a threat to local fishing fleets into a menace against international shipping during 2006–2009, eventually costing global trade an estimated $6.6–6.9 billion a year and driving Gulf of Aden insurance premiums up tenfold. It took a sustained multinational naval presence, not a single country's ships, to restore free passage. Italy's solo deployment to Bab el-Mandeb echoes the earlier piracy era's opening moves, when individual navies acted before a coordinated international task force followed; the risk is that a chokepoint this important to global energy markets needs more than one flag protecting it.
> _[Wikipedia: Piracy off the coast of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piracy_off_the_coast_of_Somalia)_

### Bank of Japan Raises Rates to 31-Year High of 1.25%, Rattling Global Markets

<!-- audio:top-story-2 -->
The Bank of Japan has raised its benchmark interest rate from 1.0% to 1.25%, the highest level in more than three decades, in a decision that reflects the central bank's confidence in Japan's economic recovery and its determination to counter persistent inflation.<sup>[1](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/18/japans-interest-rate-hiked-to-31-year-high-at-1-25-as-inflation-rises)</sup> The move follows months of gradual policy normalisation and signals Japan's clearest step yet away from the ultra-accommodative monetary policy that has defined its economy for a generation.

The rate rise carries immediate consequences well beyond Japan, sending ripples through currency and equity markets as investors reassess yen carry-trade positions built up over years of near-zero rates.<sup>[1](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/18/japans-interest-rate-hiked-to-31-year-high-at-1-25-as-inflation-rises)</sup> Other central banks are expected to watch Tokyo's next moves closely as they weigh their own paths out of inflation-fighting policy regimes.

> **Historical Parallel: The Volcker Shock** (1979–1981)
> When Paul Volcker's Federal Reserve confronted double-digit US inflation, it pushed the federal funds rate from 11% in 1979 to a peak of 20% in June 1981, in a deliberate break from years of loose monetary policy. The policy worked: US inflation fell from a peak of 14.8% in March 1980 to under 3% by 1983, but at the cost of the 1980–1982 recession and unemployment above 10%. Japan's climb to a 31-year-high rate is far more gradual and the starting inflation problem much milder, but the underlying tension is the same one Volcker faced — global markets built on cheap money have to relearn how to price risk once a major central bank decides accommodation is over.
> _[Wikipedia: Paul Volcker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Volcker)_

### Zelenskiy Arrives in Dublin as Ireland Opens Its EU Presidency

<!-- audio:top-story-3 -->
President Volodymyr Zelenskiy has landed in Dublin ahead of the formal opening ceremony for Ireland's European Union presidency, its first since 2004.<sup>[1](https://www.breakingnews.ie/ireland/)</sup> His presence at the opening underscores the EU's continued solidarity with Ukraine and gives Dublin an early, high-profile platform to set the tone for a presidency built around European security. The visit offers a chance to discuss ongoing EU aid packages and Ukraine's path toward membership, at a moment when Ireland is also hosting EU finance ministers and Central Bank governors for informal talks on the bloc's energy crisis and Middle East instability.

Taken together, the Zelenskiy visit and the finance ministers' gathering place Dublin, briefly, at the centre of the EU's response to two of its biggest current strains: the war in Ukraine and the energy pressures radiating from the Middle East.

> **Historical Parallel: Sustaining an Ally Through a Long War**
> Zelenskiy's presence at an EU presidency-opening ceremony sits within a long pattern of formal Western institutions using set-piece moments to signal continuity of support to an ally under sustained attack. The Marshall Plan committed the United States to years of economic reconstruction aid for a Europe exhausted by war, and the earlier Lend-Lease Act extended matériel to allies before the US had formally entered the conflict, in both cases turning symbolic gestures of solidarity into durable multi-year commitments. Ireland's presidency opening, with Zelenskiy on the stage, functions the same way for Ukraine now — a ceremonial moment intended to signal that European support is not going to lapse as the war grinds into its sixth year.

## World

<!-- audio:world -->
Russia's ongoing war in Ukraine continues to cast a long shadow over the wider world, most visibly in mounting evidence of how Moscow treats Ukrainian prisoners of war. Russia is believed to be holding thousands of Ukrainian soldiers and civilians, and reports describe Russian forces contacting captives' family members on social media to demand they conduct espionage, such as checking security at key locations or joining anti-government demonstrations.<sup>[1](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/16/nx-s1-5904423/russia-seizes-ukrainian-pows-then-it-demands-favors-from-their-relatives)</sup> Ukraine's Human Rights Ombudsman has documented torture affecting 95% of Ukrainian POWs and at least 337 executions, a humanitarian record that continues to weigh on the diplomacy surrounding any eventual settlement.

Elsewhere, South Korea's President Lee Jae-myung has ruled out deploying troops to the Middle East, a clear statement of Seoul's intent to avoid direct involvement in regional conflicts even as it maintains its broader alliance commitments.<sup>[2](https://www.reuters.com)</sup> The decision reflects a wider pattern of states drawing careful lines around which conflicts they will and will not enter militarily.

In Latin America, Colombia is grappling with a sharp rise in drone attacks by armed groups, with incidents more than doubling in the first half of 2026.<sup>[3](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/18/drone-attacks-by-colombian-armed-groups-more-than-double-in-early-2026)</sup> The trend shows how unmanned-systems warfare, once the preserve of state militaries, is spreading to non-state armed groups, putting civilians caught between them and government forces at growing risk.
